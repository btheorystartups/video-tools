# Temporal Intelligence System

Temporal intelligence adds deterministic sequence-level editorial analysis on top of continuity, discourse, visual structure, recommendations, and revision graph outputs.

Generated artifacts live in `sequence/` and include:

- `sequence_structure.json`
- `scene_hierarchy.json`
- `temporal_rhythm.json`
- `narrative_phase_model.json`
- `editorial_pressure.json`
- `recommendation_dependencies.json`
- `revision_propagation.json`
- `counterfactual_simulation.json`
- `revision_forecasting.json`
- `epistemic_stability.json`
- `sequence_summary.json`

The system models segment groups, pacing waves, narrative phases, pressure accumulation, recommendation interactions, proposal-only propagation estimates, and epistemic stability. It uses transcript timing, segment rates, marker counts, continuity outputs, discourse outputs, visual boundary evidence, recommendation metadata, and historical outputs when present.

Temporal, propagation, comparative, fingerprint, and semantic-equivalence-scaffolding outputs are deterministic editorial-analysis heuristics intended for explainability and human review. They are not semantic truth systems, identity-recognition systems, predictive systems, or autonomous editorial agents.

Limitations:

- No embeddings, semantic transformers, vector databases, CLIP, LLM calls, cloud APIs, Resolve calls, or media mutation are used.
- Counterfactual and forecast outputs are conservative review-priority estimates, not predictions.
- Narrative phases are structural labels inferred from timing, markers, pacing, callbacks, and continuity evidence, not semantic understanding.

Corpus hardening reuses temporal intelligence to detect recurring pressure patterns, pacing archetypes, review queue priorities, and release-candidate governance risks across a larger local video set.
